## 🟡 Governing law is New York or Delaware: **YELLOW**

**Why:** The agreement is governed by Texas law, which is another US state and not New York or Delaware, so under the rubric this is yellow rather than green. No non-US or missing governing law clause exists, so it is not red.

**Cited excerpt** (c50):

> 25. It is mandatory that any controversy or claim arising out of or relating to the Agreement or the breach thereof shall be subject to the jurisdiction of the State of Texas and the venue shall be exclusively in Collin County, Texas for resolution.

## 🟡 Counterparty cannot assign without Client's consent: **YELLOW**

**Why:** Section 23 requires the Counterparty (Subcontractor) to obtain EDGE's prior consent to assign, which matches the golden rule's core protection. However, the Counterparty is given a carve-out allowing assignment of Work to a subsidiary operating entity without consent, which is a weakness. Client (EDGE) has no express affiliate or successor carve-out, and the clause does not address Client's own assignment rights. Also, the agreement's Section 24 binding effect language refers to permitted assignments without further restriction. Because the consent requirement exists but the Counterparty has an unconsented subsidiary carve-out and there is no Client carve-out, this falls under yellow.

**Cited excerpt** (c48):

> 23. ASSIGNMENT   Subcontractor shall not assign any right or interest under this Agreement (excepting monies due, or to become due) or delegate or subcontract any Work or other obligation to be performed or owed under this Agreement without prior consent of EDGE. Notwithstanding, Subcontractor may delegate or assign Work under this Agreement to a subsidiary operating entity. All Work performed by Subcontractor's Lower- tier Subcontractors shall be deemed Work performed by Subcontractor.

## 🔴 Mutual liability cap with standard carve-outs: **RED** (missing)

**Why:** The agreement contains no limitation of liability clause capping either party's aggregate liability at fees paid or payable over a period. Sections on indemnification (13), warranty (17), and insurance (9) impose broad uncapped obligations on Subcontractor, and no cap appears anywhere in the document. Because a cap is expected for a mutual services arrangement and none exists, and Client's exposure is therefore uncapped while the counterparty's is also uncapped, the rule is not met.

## 🔴 Client may audit counterparty's records: **RED** (missing)

**Why:** The agreement contains no clause granting Client (EDGE, the customer/recipient side here) a right to audit or inspect Subcontractor's books and records. Section 8 grants inspection of Work only, not financial or business records, and Section 9 only gives a right to review insurance policies on request. No audit right exists, so this is red/missing.

## 🟡 Client may terminate for convenience: **YELLOW**

**Why:** Client has a termination-for-convenience right of 30 days' notice, which is within the 90-day green threshold. However, the right is mutual, since either party may terminate on the same notice, and it applies only after the Initial Term ends (February 16, 2019). Under the rubric, a mutual right is yellow. No Client-only convenience right exists during the Initial Term, and termination for default in Section 11 is not a convenience right.

## 🔴 Client owns deliverables and work product: **RED** (missing)

**Why:** The agreement is a subcontracting and services agreement for field work, but it has no clause assigning ownership of deliverables or work product to EDGE (Client). The only ownership language, in Section 35 (c66), covers Confidential Information, not Work product. Nothing vests IP in the Work or in deliverables, so the golden rule is not met.

## 🔴 Client may terminate on counterparty change of control: **RED** (missing)

**Why:** The agreement contains no change-of-control provision for the counterparty (FTE/Subcontractor). Assignment and delegation require EDGE's (Client's) consent, but nothing addresses a change of control of Subcontractor such as merger, acquisition, or transfer of voting securities, and there is no right for EDGE to terminate on such an event. The only related protection is anti-assignment, which does not capture a change of control by equity transfer. Termination rights exist only for default under Section 11 and for expiry on 30 days' notice under Section 1, which are not change-of-control triggers. Thus there is no change-of-control protection, which fits red/missing.
